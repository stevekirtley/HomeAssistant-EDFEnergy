    Panel Tabs (beta)#

    The EDF Energy panel is a small dashboard: the page you already know is the Overview tab, and four more sit alongside it.

    It is on by default from 19.3.0. Turning it off puts the panel back to exactly the single screen it has always been - no tab bar, nothing moved. If you had turned it off while it was opt-in, it stays off: the new default only decides for people who never said either way.

    Turning it on and off#

    From the panel, which is the quickest way:

    1. Open the EDF Energy panel and scroll to the bottom of the Overview
    2. Flip Dashboard tabs (beta)

    Or from the settings, if you prefer:

    1. Go to Settings > Devices & services > EDF Energy
    2. Click Configure on your account entry
    3. Tick or untick Show the dashboard tabs (beta) and submit

    Both write the same stored setting, so they always agree - there is no separate per-browser preference to get out of step. The switch is at the foot of the Overview whichever way it is set, so the way back is always in the same place.

    Changing it takes effect immediately and does not reload the integration: nothing in the backend reads this setting, so your entities are not disturbed. Changing any other option still reloads, as it always did.

    Administrators only

    This writes integration configuration, so only an administrator can change it. Everyone else sees how it is set and is told why they cannot change it.

    The tabs#

    Tab What is on it
    Overview Everything that was on the panel before, unchanged: the car controls, the schemes, and the off-peak and dispatch history
    Charging Just the car: the smart charge controls, the off-peak windows and the dispatch windows
    Usage The consumption chart
    Predictor Whether free electricity looks likely, from the Power Perks Predictor
    Perks The schemes - World Cup, Flextras free hours, free electricity sessions - and your referrals
    Account The money: balance, statements, transactions, refunds, and your tariffs and meters

    Nothing was taken off the Overview to make room. The Charging and Perks tabs are cuts of the same cards, so a change in one place shows up in both.

    Predictor#

    powerperkspredictor.app works out whether EDF are likely to give away free electricity, from the day-ahead auction and the week's weather. This tab shows tomorrow's call, the week ahead with the reasoning behind each day, and how the predictions have actually scored. The site itself is linked at the top of the tab - it is the full thing, and it works from anywhere.

    Nothing is fetched until you open this tab

    There is no coordinator behind this tab and no entity: the feeds are read when the tab is opened and cached for a quarter of an hour, so flicking between tabs does not keep asking. An install whose owner never opens the tab never contacts the site at all. The artwork is loaded from the site by your browser, so opening the tab is a visit.

    Nothing from it is written to an entity, an attribute or the recorder. It is somebody else's forecast rather than anything belonging to your account, so there is nothing here to automate against - use the free electricity sensors for that.

    Account#

    At a glance#

    Balance, the last statement, the next payment and how much EDF would refund. These four are also published as sensors - see Billing sensors below.

    Statements#

    Every statement on the account, newest first, with the period it covers, what it charged and the balance it closed on. Open fetches the PDF from EDF and opens it in a new tab.

    Statement links are not stored

    EDF hand out a short-lived signed link rather than a permanent URL. It is fetched at the moment you press Open and expires within the hour, so nothing is cached, written to an attribute, or kept anywhere in Home Assistant. Pressing Open again gets a fresh one.

    Transactions#

    The account ledger - charges, payments, credits and refunds - signed so it reads like a statement rather than a column of positive numbers. EDF report every amount positive and leave the direction to the entry's type, so the integration applies the sign.

    The running balance after each entry is shown underneath the amount, which makes it possible to check the ledger adds up.

    Refunds#

    How much of your credit balance EDF will actually refund, any past requests and their status, and a box to request one.

    EDF saying no is a caveat, not a block

    EDF publish an eligibility flag and a reason, and the panel shows the reason when they say no - but it still lets you make the request. The flag has proved unreliable: on one account it read HAS_NOT_GIVEN_READINGS_RECENTLY while EDF went on to accept and pay two requests made anyway. Whether to refuse is EDF's decision when the request reaches them, and a request they do refuse is simply declined at their end. Progress shows up under past requests either way.

    The refundable amount is not your balance

    EDF only refund what has been paid in by direct debit, which can be a small fraction of a credit balance. The panel will not let you ask for more than EDF quote, and neither will the websocket command behind it.

    Requesting a refund takes two presses: the first arms the button and tells you what it is about to do, the second sends it. It is admin-only, and the amount is checked against EDF's own maximum again on the Home Assistant side before anything leaves. Each request carries an idempotency key built from the account, the amount and the day, so a double press is the same request rather than a second refund.

    Statuses seen so far run REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → PAID.

    Tariffs and meters#

    Every electricity and gas meter point on the account, with its MPAN or MPRN, meter serial, make and firmware, and whether EDF consider it smart.

    Underneath each meter point is its tariff history: the agreement in force marked Live, and every agreement that has ended beneath it. This comes out of the account information the integration already holds, so it costs no extra calls to EDF.

    Note

    EDF's API gives the integration tariff and product codes, not the name the tariff is marketed under. EDF_EV_FIX_GOELEC_18M_HH is the product EDF call "Go Electric 18m". The full tariff code is shown underneath, which is the one to match against a bill.

    Perks#

    Referrals#

    Your referral link and reward amounts, how many people have joined on it, and the list of them with each one's status - EDF's own wording, so "Paid" and "Switch in progress" appear as they do in the app.

    Referral names are not stored

    These are other people's names. They are fetched from EDF while the tab is open and rendered, and that is all - there is no sensor, no attribute and no recorder row holding them.

    Usage#

    One chart covering every meter on the account - electricity import, export and gas together - in kWh or in pounds, by Day (a month at a time), Month (a year at a time) or Year.

    The cost view uses the _includingsc statistics, so it includes the standing charge and matches your bill. Export has no standing charge, so it uses the plain cost statistic.

    Today will always look empty

    The chart is built from the same daily statistics as the energy dashboard, and EDF publish readings one to two days behind. The current day, and often yesterday, will have nothing against them. That is EDF's data arriving late, not a gap in your meter.

    It also means a brand new install has nothing to chart until the first day's statistics land. Days from before you installed the integration are filled in on startup, up to a fortnight back.

    Billing sensors#

    Four sensors come with this, and unlike the tabs they are not behind the beta flag - they are created whichever way the flag is set: account balance, last bill amount, next payment amount and refund status. See Billing entities for what each one carries and how to read it.

    Supporting the work#

    At the very foot of the Overview is a short card with the ways to support development: Ko-fi and GitHub Sponsors, and referral links for Fuse Energy, Axle Energy and Monzo. Most of them credit you as well - Fuse is currently £100 each - and they are only worth anything to people who were going to sign up anyway.

    Fuse have no per-person link, so the card calls out the friend code to use at sign up; the credit comes from the code rather than from the link.

    It is on the Overview only, there is nothing fetched behind it, and none of it is expected - using the integration and reporting what breaks is support enough.

    Why it is beta#

    The layout is expected to change between releases. If something looks wrong, please raise an issue with a screenshot rather than assuming it is meant to look that way.
